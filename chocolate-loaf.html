The Chocolate Loaf That Lied to Me

Fail of the moment · Chocolate sourdough

The recipe said follow these times and temperatures.
I did exactly that.

When I pulled it out, the crust looked the part — deep chocolate brown, a few chips showing on the surface, the kind of color that makes you think “okay, this one might actually be good.” I set it on the rack feeling reasonably optimistic.

Then I cut it open.

The center was still wet. Not slightly underdone. Properly gummy and pasty in the middle, even though the outside had already committed to being done. You can see it in the photos — the outer crumb has some structure, but the middle never caught up.

This is the frustrating kind of fail. Nothing looked wrong while it was baking. The oven was at the right temperature. The timer went off when it was supposed to. And yet the loaf still managed to surprise me.

Working theory

Chocolate (especially with the extra fat and sugar from the chips) seems to slow down how heat moves through the dough. The outside sets and browns on schedule while the inside takes its time. What looks finished on the surface can still be underbaked in the center.

What I’ll try next time

I’m going to start trusting internal temperature more than the clock (aiming for around 205–210°F in the middle), and I may drop the oven temperature a bit so the crust doesn’t race ahead of the crumb.

Not my finest work. But now I know.
